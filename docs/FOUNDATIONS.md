## Ten implementation stages and acceptance criteria

These are review boundaries, not equal amounts of work or promises of one-turn completion.
Work through one bounded construction at a time; a stage may require several sessions.
Lie III in particular is a substantial project. Later stages remain planned until their
declarations compile without placeholders and their hypotheses are reviewed.

### 1. Local sections and smooth descent — implemented and checked

Prove that a map with local smooth right inverses detects smoothness of maps out of its
target. Then construct such sections from suitable submersion normal forms. This directly
supports the `smooth_descend` field of `HomogeneousQuotientRealization`.

Initial implementation: [LocalSections.lean](../LieGroups/Manifold/LocalSections.lean).
It defines `LieGroups.Manifold.HasContMDiffLocalSections` and proves surjectivity, descent of
`C^n` regularity, and the composition criterion for a `C^n` projection. It uses only Mathlib,
works over a nontrivially normed field, and allows different source/target model spaces.
It assumes the local sections.

[SubmersionLocalSections.lean](../LieGroups/Manifold/SubmersionLocalSections.lean) constructs
a local section through any given point at which Mathlib's `IsSubmersionAt` holds, and
deduces local sections everywhere for a surjective submersion. The source model is
boundaryless. This normal-form consequence needs neither finite-dimensionality nor
completeness, and works over a nontrivially normed field. It does not derive the normal
form from a differential condition, construct quotient manifolds, or discharge the
current global realization assumptions.

The statement anchors are Lee, Theorem 4.26 (the forward direction), and Theorem 4.29.
The chart-normal-form input is supplied by Stage 2 under its differential hypotheses.

Reproduction: `lake build`, then `lake env lean scripts/AuditFoundations.lean`.

Acceptance: the section takes the chosen target point to the prescribed source point,
is `C^n` there, and satisfies the right-inverse identity on a neighborhood. Build and audit
both new declarations, and keep them independent of BF–RF code.
Checked on 2026-09-26: `lake build` succeeds (3913 jobs), and all five foundation results
report only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound` as axiom dependencies.

### 2. Differential criteria and local product charts — implemented and checked

Bridge a split-surjective differential to the submersion normal form in the required
boundaryless setting, using existing inverse-function tools where possible. Establish
the finite-dimensional real specialization and identify every completeness/regularity
hypothesis rather than silently moving between differential and normal-form definitions.
Principal anchor: Lee, Theorem 4.12, specifically its submersion case.

Acceptance: local sections can be obtained from the differential criterion that will be
proved for a quotient projection, without assuming the desired charts.

First milestone implemented in
[SubmersionDifferential.lean](../LieGroups/Manifold/SubmersionDifferential.lean):

- Over `ℝ` or `ℂ` (`RCLike`), for a complete source normed space and `n ≠ 0`,
  `exists_contDiffAt_localProduct_of_rightInverse` constructs an open partial homeomorphism
  from a `C^n` map at a point and a continuous linear right inverse to its differential.
  Its coordinates are `(f z, z - R (A z))`, with the second coordinate valued in `ker A`.
  The map and its inverse are `C^n` at the base points, and `f` becomes the first projection.
- `exists_contDiffAt_localSection_of_rightInverse` fixes the kernel coordinate to construct
  a local section taking `f x` to `x`. The finite-dimensional-target corollary
  `exists_contDiffAt_localSection_of_surjective` requires only surjectivity of the differential.
  Finite-dimensional real source spaces satisfy the completeness assumption automatically.
- `hasContMDiffLocalSections_of_surjective_splitDifferential` and
  `hasContMDiffLocalSections_of_surjective_differential` connect the pointwise constructions
  to Stage 1 for surjective `C^n` maps of normed spaces, giving access to smooth descent.

The proof reuses Mathlib's `ContinuousLinearEquiv.equivOfRightInverse`,
`ContDiffAt.toOpenPartialHomeomorph`, and `ContDiffAt.to_localInverse`.
It adds no new assumptions to the existing Stage 1 declarations.

The neighborhood and manifold milestone is now implemented:

- `exists_contDiffOn_localInverse` and `exists_contDiffOn_localProduct_of_rightInverse`
  assume `ContDiffOn` on an open set and produce `C^n` maps on the entire open source and target.
  This includes `n = ∞`; no inference from pointwise `C^∞` regularity to a single smooth
  neighborhood is used.
- [SubmersionCriterion.lean](../LieGroups/Manifold/SubmersionCriterion.lean) proves
  `isSubmersionAt_of_rightInverse_mfderiv` by constructing charts in the maximal atlas.
  Its hypotheses are `RCLike 𝕜`, `CompleteSpace E`, `I.Boundaryless`, `IsManifold I n M`,
  `IsManifold J n N`, `ContMDiff I J n f`, `n ≠ 0`, and a continuous linear right inverse
  to `mfderiv I J f x`. No boundaryless assumption on the target model is needed.
- `isSubmersionAt_of_surjective_mfderiv` replaces the right inverse with surjectivity
  when the target model is finite-dimensional. In particular, it applies to smooth maps
  of finite-dimensional real manifolds without boundary; source completeness is then automatic.
- `exists_contMDiffAt_localSection_of_surjective_mfderiv` gives the prescribed-point section.
  `hasContMDiffLocalSections_of_surjective_split_mfderiv` and
  `hasContMDiffLocalSections_of_surjective_mfderiv` provide the smooth-descent interface
  when the map is also surjective.

Statement comparison: Lee's rank theorem concerns smooth maps between finite-dimensional
real manifolds of constant rank. We implement its full-rank submersion conclusion from
surjectivity at the chosen point, not the arbitrary constant-rank theorem. Combining this
with Stage 1 gives the forward local-section implication of Theorem 4.26 and the descent
criterion of Theorem 4.29. The Lean results additionally allow nonzero finite regularity,
complex scalars, and complete infinite-dimensional source models with split differentials.
Prerequisites are Mathlib's inverse function theorem, openness of the continuous linear
equivalences, finite-dimensional linear splitting, and the chart/tangent-space APIs.

Checked on 2026-09-27: `lake build` succeeds (2414 jobs); all seventeen audited declarations
report only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.
[CheckSubmersionCriterion.lean](../scripts/CheckSubmersionCriterion.lean) checks the nonlinear
map `(u, v) ↦ u + v²`: submersion normal forms, sections through arbitrary points, and smooth descent.
Reproduce with `lake env lean scripts/CheckSubmersionCriterion.lean` after the build and axiom audit.

The Stage 2 acceptance criterion is met for the stated setting. The smooth structure and
differential of a future homogeneous quotient projection must still be constructed in Stage 6.

### 3. One-parameter subgroups and exponential map

Use integral-curve existence and uniqueness to construct one-parameter subgroups,
their relation to left-invariant fields, and the Lie exponential. Prove the differential
at zero and the local inverse properties needed later. Audit existing Mathlib and
DifferentialGeometry APIs before choosing definitions. Principal anchors: Warner and Lee.

Acceptance: a proved exponential construction and local chart theorem for arbitrary
finite-dimensional real Lie groups, independent of a matrix representation.

### 4. Differentials of homomorphisms and adjoint compatibility

Prove that differentiating a smooth group homomorphism gives a Lie-algebra homomorphism,
with identity, composition, exponential, and adjoint compatibility. Reuse Mathlib's Lie
algebra conventions. Record left/right action and bracket-sign conventions explicitly.
Principal anchor: Warner; cross-check Helgason.

Acceptance: the differential construction supplies the compatibility currently packaged
as hypotheses in `IntegratedLieEquiv` and related adapters.

### 5. Frobenius and integration of Lie subalgebras

Develop the appropriate immersed/embedded Lie-subgroup interfaces, integration of Lie
subalgebras, and connected immersed Lie-subgroup uniqueness, using the appropriate
involutive distribution and Frobenius theorem. Principal anchors: Lee and Warner.

Review the existing `subgroupTangentSet` and `UniqueConnectedIsotropySubgroup` interfaces
before using a standard uniqueness theorem: the latter quantifies over connected abstract
subgroups using ambient curve velocities. Identify and prove the required regularity
conditions, or adjust the interface to the appropriate class of Lie subgroups. Do not
silently identify this predicate with the usual immersed-subgroup theorem. Acceptance:
the existence and uniqueness conclusions quantify over the correct regularity class.

### 6. Closed subgroups and homogeneous quotient manifolds

Prove the closed-subgroup theorem and, for a closed Lie subgroup, construct the smooth
manifold `G/K`, its smooth transitive action, quotient projection, local sections, and
tangent-space identification. Use Stage 1 to prove smooth descent. The construction
must not assume the quotient smooth structure or its descent property. Principal anchor:
Warner; cross-check Lee and Helgason. Check separation and countability hypotheses.

Closedness is not automatic for an integrated subalgebra. Retain the necessary closedness
condition on admissible initial homogeneous data, and prove its preservation under the
integrated isomorphisms. This is distinct from proving that a closed subgroup is a Lie subgroup.

Acceptance: supply the existing quotient interface from ordinary closed Lie-subgroup data.

### 7. Lie II and smooth covering groups

For finite-dimensional real Lie groups/algebras, provide a general Lie-homomorphism
integration theorem with its simply connected source hypothesis and uniqueness. Construct
the required smooth group structure on a universal cover rather than assuming it. Derive
integration of Lie-algebra equivalences with the prescribed differential. Principal anchor:
Warner; cross-check Knapp.

Acceptance: construct `IntegratedLieEquiv` from a Lie-algebra equivalence between supplied
simply connected finite-dimensional real Lie groups.

### 8. Lie III and simply connected integration

Construct a simply connected Lie group integrating every finite-dimensional real Lie
algebra. Principal anchor: Knapp, Appendix B, Theorem B.7. Audit its Levi/solvable/semidirect
product route against possible local-group/BCH or representation routes, including all
algebraic prerequisites, before selecting the formal proof route.

Do not treat this as a small adapter change or promise a schedule before that audit.
Quotient theory on already given groups can progress before this milestone is complete.

Acceptance: construct `IntegratedLieGroup` with its Lie-algebra identification, with no
externally supplied integrating group or placeholder. This does not imply that an arbitrary
integrated isotropy subalgebra is closed.

### 9. Invariant metrics and infinitesimal actions

Construct smooth invariant Riemannian metrics from positive-definite isotropy-invariant
inner products; prove well-definedness, smoothness, invariance, and the origin formula.
Relate infinitesimal isotropy invariance to the needed group invariance under appropriate
connectedness hypotheses. Construct the fundamental vector fields of a smooth action and
prove their value, bracket-sign, and Killing identities.

Principal anchors: Kobayashi–Nomizu I for the bundle/connection framework and II for
homogeneous geometry; cross-check Helgason. Acceptance: these results supply the current
metric and `F` inputs, rather than only comparing supplied metrics or assuming field identities.

### 10. Assemble the BF–RF application and review the global statement

Construct the initial realization and the family required by `GlobalBFRealization`, using
the preceding results. Replace its separately supplied connected-isotropy uniqueness and
smooth-descent inputs by proved consequences. Instantiate the construction with nontrivial
isotropy, as well as a simple example, to check that the interface covers homogeneous spaces
beyond the Lie-group case.

State the resulting global BF–RF theorem with standard admissibility assumptions and an
actual existence conclusion for the required realizations. Review separately whether the
intended classical statement needs joint time-smoothness; pointwise diffeomorphisms alone
do not establish it. Ricci-flow PDE existence and maximal-time claims remain separate tasks.
The application statement remains anchored in Lauret's journal article. Acceptance:
the realizations exist under reviewed admissibility hypotheses; build and audit the resulting
theorem, update the paper's scope, and review the examples before release preparation resumes.

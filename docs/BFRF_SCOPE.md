# BF–RF scope after the Lie-theoretic foundations

This review records the mathematical scope of the optional BFRF adapters in
`compat/BFRF`. They are checked against the sibling BFRF sources by
`scripts/check_bfrf_compatibility.py`, without changing either project's
dependency pins. The BFRF manuscript's earlier supplied-realization statements
remain valid, but no longer describe all the companion library's constructions.

## Initial geometric existence and admissibility

The initial algebraic data are finite-dimensional real spaces `K` and `P`, an
inner product on `P`, and a bracket on `K × P` satisfying Jacobi. The isotropy
subspace is a Lie subalgebra when `[K,K] ⊆ K`; the curvature and flow comparison
also use reductivity `[K,P] ⊆ P` and skew-adjoint isotropy on `P`.

Lie III constructs a simply connected Hausdorff integration. The definition
`IsAdmissible A hKK` asks that the canonical subgroup generated by exponentials
of the isotropy Lie algebra be closed in that integration. Closedness is a
separate global condition; it is not deduced from the algebraic splitting and
skew-adjointness assumptions.

Under admissibility, `integratedIsotropySubgroupOfAdmissible` constructs the
closed connected isotropy with its actual ambient-curve tangent identity.
The quotient charts, local smooth sections and descent, normalized smooth
invariant metric, and infinitesimal Killing realization are then constructed.
The fundamental fields have the required values at the origin and satisfy
the negative Lie-bracket convention used by BFRF. Their geometric Ricci
operator agrees with the algebraic operator under the reductivity assumption.

Connected **closed** isotropy subgroups with this tangent identity are unique.
The legacy `UniqueConnectedIsotropySubgroup` quantifies over arbitrary abstract
connected subgroups without closedness or a supplied smooth presentation.
The new construction uses actual transported isotropy compatibility instead
of assuming that stronger predicate.

## Global correspondence and the remaining analytic input

`ConstructedGlobalBFRealization` records target integrations, closed connected
isotropy, regular quotients, integrated block changes, and normalized invariant
metrics. Their existence is an output. The original `GlobalBFRealization`
interface remains available in BFRF and keeps its original hypotheses.

The evolution endpoint
`homogeneous_global_bf_rf_of_invariant_ricci_flow`, in
`compat/BFRF/GlobalCorrespondence.lean`, takes an actual supplied invariant
Ricci-flow family, its metric variation equation, the normalized initial
metric, and the stated Ricci-operator continuity hypotheses. It constructs the
moving frame and compatible global target realizations, and proves the bracket
ODE, both frame ODEs, equivariance, the differential formula at the origin,
and the metric pullback identity. This strengthens the earlier conclusion
quantifying over supplied global realization records to an existence conclusion
for constructed records.

The combined endpoint `lauret_global_bf_rf_of_admissible_bracket` starts from
the bracket and canonical closed-isotropy condition, constructs an initial
realization, and applies this conclusion to every normalized invariant actual
Ricci flow on that realization satisfying the stated analytic hypotheses.

The construction does not prove Ricci-flow PDE existence, equality of maximal
existence intervals, normalized-flow results, or geometric convergence. An
arbitrary bracket-flow solution alone is not an input from which it constructs
a Ricci-flow solution.

## What joint time-smoothness means here

The coherent construction uses the original group and quotient manifold at
every time. The group Lie-algebra identification changes by the block frame;
the quotient tangent identification changes by its inverse. The quotient
carrier, atlas, projection, action, and local sections stay fixed. In this
presentation the comparison diffeomorphisms are the identity on the common
quotient manifold.

`RegularHomogeneousQuotientRealization.contMDiff_blockTransportDiffeomorph`
therefore proves joint smoothness of the actual map `(t,x) ↦ φ_t(x)` on
`ℝ × M`. This is a theorem about the explicitly chosen common-carrier family;
it does not infer joint smoothness merely from separate smoothness of
independently chosen maps into unrelated time-indexed target types. No frame
regularity is needed for this identity comparison map. Regularity of the
time-dependent metrics and the frame itself remains governed by the hypotheses
and conclusions of the evolution theorem.
`GlobalBFRFCorrespondence.targetMetric_eq` identifies the target metric on the
common carrier with the supplied Ricci-flow metric at each time, so this
target family inherits any time regularity of the supplied flow.

## Examples reviewed

`scripts/CheckBFRFGlobalExamples.lean` constructs the additive integration of
the zero bracket and the coordinate isotropy subgroup `K × {0}`. The script
proves its closedness, connectedness, actual tangent identity, and admissibility;
it constructs the normalized metric and coherent global family.
Lie II also transports this isotropy to the unrestricted Lie III constructor,
proving canonical admissibility and instantiating the final initial-realization
existence theorem.

- `K = 0`, `P = ℝ` checks the Lie-group case.
- `K = ℝ`, `P = ℝ` has a proper nontrivial isotropy subgroup and a nontrivial
  quotient. This stabilizer is ineffective: its action fixes every quotient
  point. The example checks positive-dimensional isotropy and quotient, without
  claiming an effective isotropy representation.

The general construction does not assume effectiveness. Classical statements
that restrict to effective presentations impose that additional condition;
the algebraic preservation results retain it when it is supplied initially.

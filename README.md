## Lean foundations

Reusable manifold and Lie-group foundations in Lean 4, developed by Ramiro Lafuente.
The initial results construct local sections and prove smooth descent. For `C^n` maps
of real or complex manifolds with complete, boundaryless source model and `n ≠ 0`,
split-surjective differentials give submersion charts and local sections. For
finite-dimensional target models, surjectivity of the differential suffices.
Left-invariant vector fields on smooth real Lie groups with complete model space and
Hausdorff boundaryless manifolds now have global integral curves. The curves through the
identity are smooth one-parameter subgroups with the prescribed initial velocity.
The Lie exponential is defined by evaluation at time one, with the usual identities
along each line. For finite-dimensional real Lie groups, it is smooth in preferred
Lie-algebra coordinates, its coordinate differential at zero is the identity, and
it is a smooth local diffeomorphism there. The smooth-dependence proof reuses a
pinned DifferentialGeometry ODE theorem.
For smooth group homomorphisms, the identity differential now commutes with
one-parameter subgroups and the Lie exponential. The adjoint linear maps from
conjugation obey their multiplication and naturality laws. Lie-bracket preservation
now gives bundled Lie-algebra homomorphisms, differentials of smooth group equivalences,
and adjoint Lie-algebra automorphisms. The bracket proof reuses a pinned
DifferentialGeometry directional-derivative identity. An optional BFRF adapter identifies
its identity differential with this construction.
Connected finite-dimensional smooth subgroup images are now determined by their
differential images. Immersed and embedded subgroup interfaces retain the intrinsic
source manifold, and embedded subgroup tangents agree with ambient curve velocities.
Every Lie subalgebra of a finite-dimensional real Hausdorff Lie group with a
boundaryless model now has a connected Hausdorff immersed integration with exactly
that tangent algebra (`nonempty_lieSubalgebraIntegration`). Its intrinsic topology
and smooth group operations are constructed from Frobenius plaques, and its image
is the subgroup generated by the subalgebra's exponentials. Connected immersed
subgroup images with the same tangent algebra are unique. The construction includes
full local involutivity, higher-rank Frobenius coordinates, the intrinsic leaf atlas,
and a proof that the inclusion is an immersion. A local flow-box theorem is also
available. Closedness of an integrated subgroup is a separate condition.

Every closed subgroup of a finite-dimensional real Hausdorff Lie group with a
boundaryless model now receives an embedded Lie-group structure on its original
subspace topology (`exists_closedLieSubgroup_presentation`). The ordinary coset
space receives a smooth structure on its original quotient topology
(`nonempty_homogeneousQuotient_of_isClosed`), with a smooth transitive action,
a quotient submersion, local smooth sections, and smooth descent. The origin
tangent space is identified with the ambient Lie algebra modulo the subgroup's
tangent subspace. The subgroup can be disconnected and need not be normal.
The quotient is Hausdorff; subgroup and quotient are second countable when the
ambient group is second countable. Closedness of an integrated subalgebra is
preserved under smooth group isomorphisms.

Lie II now constructs the unique smooth group homomorphism integrating any prescribed
Lie-algebra homomorphism from a simply connected finite-dimensional real Lie group
(`nonempty_lieHomomorphismIntegration`, `existsUnique_smoothHom_of_lieHom`). The source
and target are Hausdorff and have boundaryless models; the target need not be connected.
A Lie-algebra equivalence between two such simply connected groups constructs a smooth
group equivalence with the prescribed differential (`nonempty_lieEquivalenceIntegration`).
The optional BFRF Lie II adapter supplies its actual `IntegratedLieEquiv` record from
the prescribed Lie-algebra equivalence.

The actual universal cover of a connected Hausdorff finite-dimensional real Lie group
also receives constructed smooth group operations (`nonempty_universalCoverLieGroup`).
The endpoint projection is a surjective smooth covering homomorphism and local
diffeomorphism, and its differential identifies the two Lie algebras. The construction
retains the based-path covering topology and is second countable when the base is.
Stage 8 constructs a Hausdorff simply connected Lie group integrating every
finite-dimensional real Lie algebra (`nonempty_lieAlgebraIntegration`,
`lieAlgebraIntegration`). No representation or integrating group is supplied.
The independent PBW theorem gives an ordered-monomial basis of the actual universal
enveloping algebra and proves that its canonical Lie map is injective. Ado supplies
a faithful finite-dimensional representation over every field of characteristic zero;
the geometric construction integrates its image and takes its smooth universal cover.
The Ado proof, including Whitehead, Weyl, and Levi, is adapted from
[Miyahara Kō's formalization](LieGroups/Algebra/Ado/README.md), with its Apache 2.0
license and attribution retained. The optional BFRF Lie III adapter constructs the
actual downstream record from a bracket and its Jacobi proof. Invariant metrics
and the final BF–RF assembly remain the next stages.
See [the ten-stage roadmap](docs/FOUNDATIONS.md).

Requires the Lean version in `lean-toolchain` and the Mathlib revision in `lakefile.toml`.

The optional [differentiation adapter](compat/BFRF/HomomorphismLieAlgebra.lean),
[embedded subgroup adapter](compat/BFRF/LieSubgroup.lean),
[closed-subgroup quotient adapter](compat/BFRF/ClosedSubgroupQuotient.lean),
[Lie II adapter](compat/BFRF/LieII.lean), and
[Lie III adapter](compat/BFRF/LieIII.lean) are checked
against the actual downstream structures. The quotient adapter constructs
`BFRF.Homogeneous.HomogeneousQuotientRealization` from supplied integrating-group
and closed-isotropy records, with an explicit Hausdorff assumption on the ambient group.
The check requires an existing
BFRF checkout with the same toolchain and dependency versions, with an up-to-date
build through `BFRF.HomogeneousLieAlgebra`. From this repository, run:

```sh
lake build DifferentialGeometry.Geometry.Curvature.CurvatureOperator.CurvatureBundling DifferentialGeometry.Geometry.Metric.LieDerivative.Cartan
python3 scripts/check_bfrf_compatibility.py ../BFRF
```

The runner recompiles BFRF's actual integration, isotropy, and homogeneous quotient
sources and the five adapters in a temporary directory, then checks examples and
axiom dependencies. It does not change the BFRF checkout or either project's dependency
pins. The adapters remain outside the core library to avoid a dependency cycle.
